Protection for unexpectedly adverse stop-loss slippage.

A stop-loss is in place, but the fill slips and the loss is larger than expected. Evaluation accounts include a compensation mechanism for this kind of adverse slippage.

The official terms treat slippage up to 10% of the realized loss as normal, without adjustment. For adverse slippage above that, they describe compensation capped at 50% of the total position loss.

This is compensation after a fill slips past the stop-loss price. It applies to evaluation accounts, not funded accounts. Eligibility and the adjustment amount for a specific trade follow the official terms.

08

Payout conditions: receiving your A-book profits.

With positive A-book P&L;, one trade can be enough for an instant payout.

The starting condition is positive A-book P&L; and eligible profit available to withdraw. Funded accounts do not require you to accumulate trading days for a payout. Even one trade can be enough if all conditions are met.

Outstanding A-book losses and PFP funded-account fees are settled first. Classic / PFP must also meet conditions such as the $50 minimum payout after profit split and fee settlement.

Sources: A-book P&L; and fee settlement ↗ No minimum funded-account trading days ↗

A disadvantage to understand before buying

At −1%, you move to B-book. Recovery profits cannot be withdrawn.

A major drawback is HIVE's AADS rule: when a funded account falls roughly 1% below its initial balance, open positions are closed and the account moves to B-book. If you expect to trade freely down to the usual maximum-loss limit and collect the profits along the way, this rule changes that picture.

Even if the account remains active, B-book trading profits are not eligible for cash payouts. First recover to the initial balance to return to A-book, then offset previous A-book losses with new A-book profits. Restoring the balance and increasing withdrawable profit are two different things.

This is particularly restrictive for strategies that allow large temporary floating losses, or for traders who want to earn withdrawable profits while recovering from losses. Decide whether your method fits this rule before choosing on a low purchase price alone.

Why still use HIVE? The experience once you have eligible profits.

The −1% condition is tough. We still want to introduce HIVE because its trading environment and payout experience are appealing. If your method can keep risk small and remain in A-book, you can request eligible profits as they arise, without waiting for the next scheduled payout day.

Coins earned on passing can reduce the price of the next account. Combined with PFP's profit-funded fees, that can let you continue with less cash upfront. It is an interesting combination. But B-book profits still do not become cash-withdrawable profits. Whether your regular method can handle the −1% switch is the deciding point.

Multiple accounts: watch overlapping instruments and copying

There are restrictions on copying trades across funded accounts and holding overlapping positions in the same instrument. Even different currency pairs can lose together because of correlation. With more accounts, manage the total risk as well as risk on each individual account.

Payouts also have a minimum and a daily cap

Classic / PFP has a $50 minimum after the profit split and fee settlement. The terms state a daily cap of $1,000 per ERC20 address. Check that limit by address, not by account count. Eligible A-book profit and fee-settlement conditions still come first.
