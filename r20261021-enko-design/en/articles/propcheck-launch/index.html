If you trade with prop firms, you've almost certainly asked yourself at least one of these:

"At the end of the day, which firms actually pay out?"
"How much of what people say on X (formerly Twitter) can I actually trust?"
"Am I going to trip over some fine-print rule and get my payout denied after the fact?"

I've wrestled with exactly these doubts myself. I'm an active challenger: I take evaluations at several prop firms, pass them, and go through the payout process for real.

Open social media and your feed is full of affiliate-driven shilling and "XX% off with my coupon!" promos. And when you do see someone posting about a denied payout or a banned account, there's no way to tell from the outside whether the firm treated them unfairly — or whether they simply broke an obscure rule that's easy for traders to miss.

A big discount and a reliable payout are two completely different things.

Put bluntly: no matter how cheap the challenge fee is, it means nothing if you can't withdraw after you pass and make a profit. On the flip side, a firm with somewhat stricter rules can be a much better place to trade if, as long as you follow the conditions, payouts are processed automatically and land within seconds.

Until now, there was nowhere you could compare firms side by side, on the same criteria, on three things: what reviews are really made of, what actually backs the business, and the disqualification conditions buried deep in the terms.

It didn't exist, so I built it. That's PROP CHECK.

In this article I'll explain why I built the site, and walk through exactly how I — as a trader — would use PROP CHECK. Whether you're about to take your first challenge or already trading accounts at several firms, bookmark it and use it as your go-to reference.

Why prop firm comparisons have been so unreliable

Most prop firm information out there has had three big problems.

1. Promotional posts and genuine first-hand experiences are mixed together

Search a firm's name on X and you'll get a flood of giveaway reposts and promo posts with referral codes. At the same time, posts from people who simply oversized their lots and blew their own account get counted as "negative reviews." With all that noise, you can't see an honest assessment of payouts and trading conditions.

2. "Broker-backed" and "on-chain" have become buzzwords

More and more firms market themselves as "safe because a major broker is behind us" or "transparent because we're Web3 / on-chain."
But a group company holding a financial license is not the same as the prop arm's payouts being guaranteed.
Likewise, "on-chain" can mean very different things: it might just mean you can deposit and withdraw in crypto, or it might mean payout decisions themselves are automated by a smart contract.

3. The most dangerous "special rules" never make it into comparison tables

Most comparison tables on roundup sites only list account size, fee, profit target, max drawdown, and daily drawdown.
But that's not where most profit deductions and disqualifications actually come from. Think of rules like:

  • "Once floating loss hits a certain percentage (1–2%), you can be penalized even if you're still within max drawdown"
  • "Re-enter in the same direction within 10 minutes and your previous loss is combined with the new trade"
  • "Post your support conversations on social media and your account is terminated and rewards forfeited"

These "step on it unknowingly and you're out" rules are routinely buried deep inside terms-of-service PDFs or FAQ sub-pages.

PROP CHECK was designed to strip away all three of these black boxes. It currently reviews and lists 30 major prop firms — both Japan-based and international — on a common set of criteria.

PROP CHECK's three pillars — and what's behind the data

The site doesn't rank firms on vague impressions.
Each firm is broken down into three criteria you'll want to check before you buy a challenge — Payout & account reports (50%), First-hand experiences on X (20%), and Operational foundation (30%) — and we publish an overall reference score out of 100 together with all of the underlying evidence for each.

Here's a closer look at how each one is compiled behind the scenes.

① Payout & account reports (50% of the score): first-hand reports with the noise stripped out

The single most important question when choosing a prop firm is: "Are people actually getting paid, and what kinds of problems are they running into?"

PROP CHECK collects a huge volume of posts from X, classifies them with AI, and then adds a manual review of the original text.
Specifically, the following posts are all excluded from the count:

  • Reposts, quotes, and replies
  • Second-hand claims (e.g. "I heard so-and-so got denied")
  • Reports that only concern affiliate commissions or fee refunds/compensation
  • Self-inflicted account blowups (reports where the trader simply hit the drawdown limit through their own trading mistakes)

Only the first-hand reports in ordinary posts (N) that remain are used as the denominator, and we show how many of each were observed: Payout received, Payout refused, BAN / account closure, Profit reduced, and Payout delayed.

And it doesn't stop at a number. Click through from any item and you can see exactly which posts were counted as evidence, so you can check the primary sources with your own eyes.

② First-hand experiences on X (20% of the score): the real mood over the last 6 months, and whether complaints are rising or falling

It's common for a prop firm's payout speed or execution quality to change dramatically within a few months. A review from a year ago saying "payouts were lightning fast" doesn't help much if payout delays suddenly started piling up last month.

That's why the review breakdown focuses mainly on posts from the last 6 months, excluding the firm's official posts, and shows the split as positive / mixed / negative in three colors.

The part I personally obsessed over most is the review breakdown itself.

Common review topics are tallied by category under a strict rule: if the same person posts the same complaint repeatedly in the same month, it counts as one (poster × month × category) — and every counted post can be viewed. That way you can see at a glance what users honestly think.

③ Operational foundation (30% of the score): a rigorous 20-item check that separates self-reported claims from external proof

For the operational foundation scoring basis, we go through each firm's official terms, published statistics, and on-chain data — and even the regulator registers and financial statements of parent companies and backing brokers — and assess them across 20 items, A1 through D4.

For example, we draw clear lines like these:

  • Published payout record: Is the firm merely self-reporting "we've paid out $XX in total" on its website, or can actual transfer transactions (date and amount) be matched against external blockchain records such as Etherscan or TRONSCAN?
  • Financial supervision of the backing organization: Can it be verified with public documents — for example FTMO's acquisition of OANDA (FCA-registered); Purple Trading (CySEC-registered), part of the same group as Fintokei (a prop firm widely used by Japanese traders); Hantec Markets (FCA-registered, with audited accounts filed at the UK's Companies House), which backs RZR Funded (formerly Hantec Trader); or the consolidated financial statements of FXIFY's backing broker's parent company (Form 10-K/A filed with the U.S. SEC)?
  • Contracts and rule changes: Can the version of the applicable terms (or a signature hash) be identified? Is there a provision preventing unfavorable rule changes from applying retroactively to past trades? Is there a clearly stated channel for appealing or requesting a review of enforcement actions?

A trader's view: how I'd use PROP CHECK in 4 steps

There are a lot of features, so here's the order I'd actually filter in — and how I'd settle on a plan — when choosing a new prop firm.

Honestly, if digging through detailed data isn't your thing, it's fine to narrow down your shortlist by simply trusting the overall reference score and its three component scores (Payout & account reports, First-hand experiences on X, Operational foundation).
All the noise filtering and terms cross-checking has already been done behind the scenes, so use those numbers as your base and work through the four steps below.

Step 1: Narrow down your candidates with filters and sorting

Don't start by looking at plan prices. Instead, use the comparison table on the home page (or the Featured firms at the top) to filter for firms that match your conditions.

  • Filter by market and plan conditions: Choose a market category such as "FX/CFD / On-chain," plus the challenge type you want ("1-Step / 2-Step / Instant"), the account currency ("USD / JPY" — JPY plans are offered by some Japan-focused firms), and the account size (e.g. $50K, or ¥5M for yen-denominated accounts). Only firms with matching plans stay in the list.
  • Use sorting: The default is "Overall score: high to low," but you can also sort by "Payout outcome: high to low," "Issue reports: low to high," and the operational foundation score ("Transparency: high to low").

Step 2: Check the review breakdown and monthly trend for recent red flags

Once you're down to a handful of firms, open each firm's detail page and look at the Reviews tab and the Monthly tab.

What to look for is simple:

  • Reviews tab: Check what sits at the top of the "📌 Top complaint" list for the last 6 months, and whether anything dangerous (payout delays, rule disputes, etc.) is showing up under "📈 Recently rising (latest 90 days)."
  • Monthly tab: Look at monthly post volume and how the three-color positive / mixed / negative bar changes over time. Check whether the red bars (negative reviews) have suddenly grown over the last 2–3 months — this is where you weed out firms whose business health or payout reviews have recently gotten worse.
  • Rule change history tab: Check how often rules change, how many recent changes made conditions worse for traders, and whether the firm has ever applied a worse rule retroactively to existing accounts.

Step 3: Avoid landmines with refusal/BAN reports and the special-rules check

If recent review trends look fine, move on to the Refusals & bans tab and the Plans & rules tab. This is the most important filter.

  • See the real causes of trouble in the Refusals & bans tab
    By default, first-hand reports of "Profit reduced / refused / BAN" are listed newest first. Open the original X posts and get a sense of what kind of trading led to the refusal or ban — a device ID match, slippage around news releases, being flagged for martingale, and so on.
  • Check special rules in Plans & rules
    Review the "easy-to-miss special rules" pulled from each firm's terms, along with "key rule application info (change history, etc.)."

This is where you check whether your own trading style (scalping, day trading, swing trading, EAs, etc.) would run into any of that firm's special rules. If a firm has rules that don't fit your approach, drop it from your list at this stage.

Step 4: Compare plans side by side among the survivors

Once you've cleared Steps 1–3 and you're left with two or three firms you're confident about — "they have a payout track record, nothing has deteriorated recently, and my strategy won't break their rules" — that's when you finally compare plan specs and value for money.

Wrapping up: step one is to survive and get your first payout

Prop firms are an attractive way to trade large capital starting from a small amount of money. But that only holds if you accurately understand the rules and structure standing between you and a payout — and choose a firm you can trust.
