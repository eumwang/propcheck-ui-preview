What a wallet actually is

When you receive prop payouts in USDT or USDC, there are basically two places to keep them: on an exchange, or in your own wallet.

An exchange account is close to leaving money in a bank. The “key” needed to move crypto is held by the exchange. If the exchange or broker freezes your account, you can’t move anything yourself.

A wallet is a tool for holding that key yourself. In an extreme case, even if a provider shuts down its service, you can still move your assets as long as you have the key. In exchange, if you lose the key, nobody can help you.

Let me cover just four terms that come up later.

TermMeaningAn everyday comparison
KeyThe information used to move your crypto. Whoever holds it is the ownerThe key to a safe
AddressA long string of letters and numbers you give someone so they can send you cryptoA bank account number
NetworkThe road crypto travels on. Even for the same USDC, there are many kinds, such as Ethereum, Arbitrum, and SolanaA railway line. If the line is different, it won’t arrive
Network feeThe fee charged every time you send. You pay it in that network’s currency (ETH for Ethereum)A ticket fare

What Tangem is

It looks like an ordinary contactless payment card. You open the app on your phone and tap the card to the back of it. The key is generated inside the card’s chip and never leaves it.

A ring-shaped version you can wear on your finger is also sold.

  • The cards come in sets of two or three. Every card holds the same key, so if you lose one you can keep using the others.
  • By default, it doesn’t use a 12-word seed phrase (a recovery passphrase). You can set one up, but it’s considered better not to create one. You don’t have to write it on paper and store it, but if you lose all the cards, nobody can recover your funds.
  • It supports more than 100 blockchains and more than 20,000 tokens (according to the official site).
  • The chip is EAL6+ certified, it is dust- and water-resistant to IP69K, and the warranty is 25 years.

It is made by Tangem AG, a Swiss company. It was reported to have received $15 million in investment from Japan’s SBI Group in 2019.

I recommend buying from the official site. As I explain later, there was an incident with another company’s wallets in which tampered devices circulated. In my case, it took about a week from ordering on the official site to delivery (shipped to Japan).

From delivery to first use

  1. Install the Tangem app on your phone. You need iOS 15 or later, or Android 6.0 or later, on a device with NFC.

  2. Tap “Scan” in the app and hold a card to the back of your phone. Taking the case off makes it easier to read.

  3. Choose “Create wallet”. This is where the key is generated inside the card.

  4. Register the remaining cards as backups.

  5. Set an access code (the equivalent of a PIN).

Register all your backups up front. The one to watch is step 4. With a 3-card set, register both of the other two cards here. If you finish after registering just one, you can’t add more later (according to the official help).

Let me say up front that no hardware wallet is “absolutely safe.” Within that, though, I think it is a strong option as a measure an individual can start easily.

Four risks to actually watch for

Lined up like this the weaknesses look like a lot, but honestly, these four are about the only ways you would lose assets in normal use.

RiskWhen it happensHow to prevent it
You approve it yourselfYou tap the card to approve a transfer to a fake site or a fake addressBefore approving, compare the recipient and network on your phone screen. Start with a small amount for a new destination
One card is stolenYou haven’t set an access code, or it is easy to guessAlways set an access code that is hard to guess
You lose all the cardsYou keep all 3 cards in one place and get hit by a fire or theftStore the 3 cards in separate places
You forget the access code with one card leftResetting the code requires two cardsIf you lose one card, move your funds to a new set soon

Tangem is not a “works if 2 of the 3 cards are together” system. Any single card can move the full balance on its own. Even if someone finds or steals a card, they can’t take your funds without the access code.

If you enter the access code wrong, from the sixth attempt on the wait grows by 1 second each time, up to 45 seconds. Avoid short, easy-to-guess codes made only of digits.

When you lose a card or your phone

SituationWhat happens
You lost your phoneNo impact. Install the app on a new phone and scan the card
You lost one cardYou can keep using the remaining cards as they are
You want to replace the lost oneYou can’t. You can’t add a card to an already-created wallet later
You forgot the access codeYou can reset it if you have two cards
You lost all the cardsWith a no-seed-phrase setup, the funds can’t be recovered

If it were me, the moment I lost one card I would buy a new set and move my funds. The lost card might be in someone’s hands, and the fewer cards remain, the bigger the four risks above get.

What has happened to Tangem in the past

I will cover the inconvenient parts, too. I could confirm two cases.

  • In December 2024, a bug in the Android app. The private key of wallets created with a seed phrase was recorded in the app’s logs, where support staff could see it. It affected people who contacted support through the app within 7 days of creating their wallet. Tangem said it was “less than 0.1% of users, with no loss of funds,” and fixed it on December 30.
  • In July 2026, researchers at rival Ledger published an attack method. It uses a laser to make the chip malfunction, which allows the password to be reset. It requires the physical card and about $250,000 of equipment. It can’t be fixed. Tangem rebutted that “the risk to ordinary users is practically nonexistent.”

How it compares with other wallets

Compared with MetaMask

When people hear “wallet,” the first name that comes to mind is probably MetaMask. It is a free app you install on your phone or PC. The biggest difference from Tangem is where the key is kept.

TangemMetaMask
Where the key is keptInside the card’s chipOn your phone or PC
Distance from the internetThe key never touches the internetThe key sits inside a device that is connected to the internet
How to recoverBackup cards12-word seed phrase
CostYou pay for the cardsFree
Best useHolding a large amountMoving small amounts quickly

MetaMask is free and quick to start. But since the key is on your phone or PC, if that device gets a virus or you type your seed phrase into a fake site, your assets get drained. In an extreme case, you are entrusting everything to the security of a single phone.

Personally, for holding a large sum like prop payouts, something that keeps the key off the internet is my main pick. I think MetaMask is plenty if you keep it separate for moving small amounts.

Compared with wallets that have a screen

The best-known hardware wallets are devices with a screen, like Ledger and Trezor. Here are the differences from Tangem.

TangemDevices with a screen (Ledger, Trezor, etc.)
Checking the transactionYou look at your phone’s screenYou look at the device’s screen. You can notice even if your phone or PC has been taken over
How to recoverBackup cards (no seed phrase by default)12–24-word seed phrase
The program insideCan’t be rewrittenCan be updated. You can fix flaws, but you have to watch out for fake updates
Battery and connectionNo battery. Tap to the phoneUSB or Bluetooth. Some models have a battery
What gets targetedThe physical card, and what is shown on the phone screenThe seed phrase

Being able to check the transaction on the device’s own screen is a clear strength of devices with a screen. But a screen doesn’t automatically mean you are safe. Incidents in which the device itself was tampered with have actually happened.

  • 2023: a tampered Trezor. A case reported by the security firm Kaspersky. The contents of a Trezor Model T bought on a peer-to-peer marketplace had been swapped, and the seed phrase was one the attacker already knew from the start. The hologram seal on the box was intact, and the screen and operation looked identical to the real thing. The funds were drained about a month after the deposit.
  • 2021: tampered Ledgers delivered to people’s homes. Ledger had a customer-data leak in 2020, and the contact details of about 270,000 people were published. A tampered Ledger Nano X, described as a “replacement,” was sent to those addresses, and the trick was to get people to enter their seed phrase in the bundled app.

Both were attacks aimed at the seed phrase. Tangem doesn’t use one by default, so the same trick doesn’t work as is. On the other hand, the weakness of having no screen remains.

What holds for every device is to buy from the official source. Tangem is no exception. With a used card, the seller may have kept a backup card holding the same key.

Tangem Pay: a top-up Visa card, too

Tangem Pay is a virtual card you top up with USDC or USDT and use at Visa merchants. You can register it in Apple Pay and Google Pay. In Japan it became available in April 2026. Availability depends on your country, so check the official page.

It is operated by Paera LLC of the US, and the cards are issued by a company called Rain. The official site states that Tangem AG does not operate it.

From signing up to using it

You need to already have a Tangem wallet. You choose the plan inside the app after identity verification.

  1. Open Tangem Pay in the Tangem app and create a virtual card.

  2. Verify your identity. You need a government-issued ID and an address.

  3. Top up with USDC or USDT.

  4. Register the card details in Google Pay or Apple Pay. Enter the confirmation code sent to your phone, and you can use it for tap payments in stores.

Once approved, the card is usually issued within 5 minutes (rarely, up to 48 hours).

The one to watch is step 3. What you can send in from outside to top up is USDC or USDT on Polygon, Base, BNB Chain, or Arbitrum. Ethereum, which many people use for USDC and USDT, is not on this list.

The official help says funds sent on an unsupported network are not returned. Stablecoins already in your Tangem wallet can be topped up through the in-app swap.

Security: easier to keep losses small

If a credit card number leaks, it can be used up to the full credit limit. Tangem Pay works differently, so you set the cap on losses yourself.

How it worksWhat it means for you
Top-up modelYou can only spend up to the balance you loaded. Even if the card details leak, no more than what you put in can be taken. The bulk of your assets stays in the Tangem wallet
You set the daily limit yourselfEnter an amount in the app and it takes effect immediately. Set it to $0 to stop payments
FreezeTap in the app to stop new payments. Your balance stays as it is, and unfreezing is instant
Quick reissueTap reissue in the app and the old card details stop working on the spot. The new virtual card usually arrives within 5 minutes
Google Pay / Apple PayEvery payment needs fingerprint or face verification. When you reissue, you just register the new card again
You can split cardsEven Basic lets you hold up to 3. You can keep one for monthly subscriptions and another for one-off payments

With a virtual card plus Google Pay, you don’t have to wait for a physical card to arrive. If you think the number might have leaked, you can freeze it on the spot and switch to a new number within minutes.

If it were me, I would keep only what the next payment needs on the card. When it is not in use, I would freeze it or set the daily limit to $0. In an extreme case, if the balance is $100, the worst you lose is $100.

There are some caveats, too.

  • Payment disputes go through Visa’s process and cost a $30 fee per case. Refunds usually take 3–14 business days.
  • The fee for reissuing a physical card is listed as $1 in the fee schedule and $5 in the help, so the two don’t agree.

I would use it only for trading expenses

If it were me, I would make Tangem Pay a card dedicated to paying trading expenses, such as prop firm challenge fees and TradingView subscriptions. Both are billed in US dollars, so no foreign-exchange fee applies, and there are few of them, so the records are easy to keep up with.

How I would use it

If it were me, I would use Tangem as “the place to keep the profits I have withdrawn.” The basic rule is: don’t let profits pile up in a broker account, and don’t leave them sitting on an exchange either.

  • Keep large amounts on Tangem
  • Split off small amounts to move quickly into MetaMask or similar
  • Store the 3 cards in separate places

Five things I would stick to

Putting everything so far together, there are five things to stick to.

  • Buy from the official site. Don’t use used or resold ones
  • Use the no-seed-phrase setup (the 2024 bug happened to wallets created with a seed phrase)
  • Choose the 3-card set and keep the cards in separate places
  • Always set an access code (6 or more characters; not digits only)
  • Always compare the recipient and network before approving

In an extreme case, if you kept all 3 cards in the same drawer at home, a fire or burglary would take all of them. The laser attack published in July 2026 also only works if the card ends up in the attacker’s hands. What you need to protect is the physical card.

Everything so far has been about where to keep your money after you have received a payout. Still, even if you have a wallet, it means nothing if the firm doesn’t pay. Whether you can get a payout is decided by which firm you choose, before the wallet. Each firm’s payout reports and records of refusals and bans are on PROP CHECK, and the rules to watch for are summarized in What to Watch Out for in Prop Firm Rules.

Check the odds of getting paid when you choose a firm, then protect what you receive yourself with Tangem. I think that order is the most reassuring.
