Wait for your setup. No extra trades to fill the calendar.

04

Three fixed-loss plans and one trailing 2STEP plan.

Classic 1-Step | Start with the fixed 6% allowance

Classic 1-Step is a useful starting point: $999 for 100K, a 10% profit target and a fixed 6% overall loss allowance. The floor stays at $94,000, so an increase in unrealized profit does not pull the breach level upward with it.

Turbo 1-Step | A lower entry fee with less room for losses

100K costs $450—$549 less than Classic 1-Step. In exchange, the loss allowance is halved to $3,000 while the target is $9,000. Bringing your usual lot sizes unchanged could feel restrictive; this is better suited to small, controlled losses.

Pro 1-Step | A middle ground on fee and loss allowance

For $699, you get a fixed 5% overall loss allowance. Both fee and allowance sit between Classic and Turbo, but the 12% profit target is the highest of the three. On 100K, you need $12,000 in profit: saving $300 on entry means earning $2,000 more than Classic requires.

Classic 2-Step | Two targets and a trailing loss floor

The fee is $749, with targets of 5% then 10%. The overall loss allowance is 8%, but it trails the equity high. If a large unrealized gain reverses, you can be close to the breach level even while still in profit. If your strategy lets winners run, compare this carefully with a fixed-loss 1STEP plan.

05

Close the profit, then request a payout. Here is the process.

You do not need to wait for a scheduled payout date. All profits are split on withdrawal and the balance returns to its initial amount. You cannot request a partial payout and leave the rest to build a larger account balance.

On-chain transparency and automated payouts are different features.

Transfers leave an on-chain record, but people process payout requests. It does not use the automated instant-transfer process found at HIVE or Hypernova. The official estimate is within 24 hours. If speed is your top priority, keep this difference in mind.

06

What to know before buying.

What can you verify on-chain?

You can follow dashboard revenue and payouts, but internally handled B-book trades and every account or accounting record are not all public. A-book and B-book records are also handled differently. Use the disclosed figures to follow the business over time.

Restrictions on cross-account hedging and repeated all-in attempts

Prohibited practices include opposite trades across multiple or external accounts, account sharing, exploiting latency, and repeatedly taking evaluations at maximum risk. Funded accounts have a combined cap of 300K.

Who might it suit?

I think Propr suits someone who wants to keep checking the business numbers after buying an account. You can follow revenue and payouts on a dashboard, rather than relying only on company posts. Try the dashboard before choosing a plan—it gives a good feel for what makes Propr different.
